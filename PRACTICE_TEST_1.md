## Section A — Multiple Choice (1 pt each, 15 pts)

Circle the single best answer.

1. The principle "the greatest happiness for the greatest number" is associated with:
   a) Kant   b) Aristotle   c) Bentham/Mill   d) Regan

2. Kant's Categorical Imperative is best summarized as:
   a) Maximize happiness  b) Act on a maxim you can will to be a universal law  c) Cultivate virtue  d) Respect sentience

3. *Telos* in Aristotle's ethics means:
   a) Pleasure  b) Duty  c) Purpose / end / function  d) Custom

4. Which thinker argues that animals are "subjects-of-a-life" with inherent value?
   a) Singer   b) Regan   c) Cohen   d) Bentham

5. *Speciesism*, as Singer uses the term, is most analogous to:
   a) Utilitarianism  b) Racism/sexism  c) Virtue ethics  d) Consequentialism

6. The 3Rs of Russell & Burch are:
   a) Rights, Respect, Responsibility  b) Replacement, Reduction, Refinement  c) Reuse, Recycle, Reduce  d) Reason, Rules, Results

7. The "F" in FAIR data principles stands for:
   a) Free  b) Findable  c) Formatted  d) Faithful

8. Haidt's Social Intuitionist Model claims that moral reasoning is mostly:
   a) Logical and primary  b) Post-hoc justification of intuitions  c) Irrelevant  d) Genetically fixed

9. The "humanity formula" of the Categorical Imperative says:
   a) Treat humanity always as an end, never merely as a means
   b) Maximize human welfare
   c) Imitate virtuous humans
   d) Humans are God's image

10. Mill's modification of Bentham was to introduce:
    a) Universal law  b) Higher vs. lower pleasures  c) The doctrine of the mean  d) Virtue ethics

11. CARE principles primarily address:
    a) Animal welfare  b) Indigenous data governance  c) Patient consent  d) Open access fees

12. Carl Cohen denies animals have rights because:
    a) They cannot suffer  b) They are not members of a moral community  c) They lack DNA  d) God forbade it

13. Descriptive ethics differs from normative ethics in that it:
    a) Tells us what we ought to do  b) Studies how people actually judge morally  c) Is religious  d) Is identical to metaethics

14. Sandel's flute example argues that the best flutes should go to:
    a) The richest bidder  b) Whoever needs them most  c) The best flute players  d) A random lottery

15. Which is **NOT** an objection to utilitarianism?
    a) It can violate individual rights
    b) It reduces all values to a single scale
    c) It is too focused on intentions and ignores consequences
    d) It can justify sacrificing minorities

## Section B — Short Answer (5 pts each, 25 pts)

Answer in 3–6 sentences each.

16. State both the **Universal Law** and **Humanity** formulations of Kant's Categorical Imperative and apply each to making a *false promise* to repay a loan.

17. Define the three Rs of animal research (**Replacement, Reduction, Refinement**) and give one concrete example of each.

18. Explain Sandel's "flute argument" and how it illustrates the role of **telos** in distributive justice.

19. Distinguish **descriptive** from **normative** ethics. Then give one way descriptive findings (e.g., Haidt or Greene) can be *relevant to* normative debate without committing the naturalistic fallacy.

20. What does **FAIR** stand for? Why is FAIR data not necessarily *open* data? Give one example.

## Section C — Essay (20 pts)

Choose ONE of the following. Write 1–2 pages.

**E1.** "Utilitarianism is the most defensible ethical framework for evaluating animal research because it takes suffering seriously without forbidding all use of animals." Critically discuss, drawing on Singer, Regan, Cohen, and the 3R framework.

**E2.** Open Science promises greater equity in research, but Ross-Hellauer warns it can entrench cumulative advantage. Explain the tension and propose two concrete policy measures (referencing FAIR and/or CARE principles) that could mitigate it.

**E3.** Compare how a utilitarian, a Kantian, and an Aristotelian would each evaluate the dilemma of using one healthy patient's organs to save five dying patients. Which view do you find most persuasive, and why?
