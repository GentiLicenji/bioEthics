# Faculty Review — PBIO 2026 Study Materials

*Reviewer: PhD-level bioethics professor, posing as external examiner.*
*Scope: all materials currently in the project.*

---

## Overall verdict

The package (study guide + two practice tests + answer keys + organized source folders) is **substantively accurate, well-scoped to the announced syllabus, and at the right cognitive level for a 2nd-year course**. Only **minor factual corrections** were needed in the study guide, and the practice tests are in an appropriate format. The structure (folders 01–07) is clean and pedagogically sound.

**Grade for the materials as a whole:** A− → A after the corrections below were applied.

---

## Corrections applied to `01_Study_Guide/README.md`

1. **Haidt's metaphor** — was inverted ("rational tail wags the emotional dog"). Haidt's actual phrase is *"the emotional dog and its rational tail"*: intuition (dog) drives reasoning (tail). **Fixed.**
2. **Mill's harm principle** — was attributed to *Utilitarianism* (1863). It is from *On Liberty* (1859); it is associated with, but not part of, the strict utilitarian doctrine. **Fixed.**
3. **Moral Foundations Theory** — listed 6 foundations as canonical. The original theory (Haidt, Graham, Joseph) has **five**; **Liberty/Oppression** is a *later candidate* sixth. **Fixed.**
4. **Greene's neuroethics result** — clarified that personal/impersonal dilemmas correlate with deontological/utilitarian *judgments* respectively (the original phrasing only mentioned brain regions). **Fixed.**
5. **Cultural/developmental variation** — added Gilligan (*ethic of care*) and unpacked the **WEIRD** acronym. **Added.**
6. **Friedman & Hendry, *Value Sensitive Design*** — present in `04_Normative_Theories/` but **not on the tested syllabus**. Flagged as **optional / off-syllabus** in the materials map; do **not** spend exam-prep time here.

## Items I deliberately left alone

- The **3Rs section** is excellent: correct attribution to Russell & Burch (1959), correct EU Directive 2010/63/EU and Czech Act 246/1992 Sb. references, correct distinction between absolute vs. relative replacement.
- **Singer / Regan / Cohen / Bateson / Ball & Norris** attributions match the Soucitně.cz article.
- **FAIR (Wilkinson 2016)** and **CARE (Carroll 2020)** are correctly stated, including the important caveat that *FAIR ≠ Open*.
- **Sandel** sections (Utilitarianism, Kant, Aristotle) faithfully reflect the source PDFs; the flute, Casey Martin, and Ford Pinto examples are accurate.

## Practice tests — format & coverage check

### `PRACTICE_TEST_1.md` (60 pts, 60 min)
- **Format:** 15× MCQ (1 pt) + 5× short answer (5 pts) + 1× essay (20 pts). Standard mid-term format. ✅
- **Coverage:** all six tested topics are touched (utilitarianism Q1/10/15; Kant Q2/9/16; Aristotle/telos Q3/14/18; descriptive ethics Q8/13/19; animal ethics + 3R Q4/5/6/12/17; Open Science / FAIR / CARE Q7/11/20). ✅
- **Calibration:** difficulty is appropriate. Q15 (NOT-question) is the only mildly tricky item — fair.
- **Verdict: hits the mark.**

### `PRACTICE_TEST_2.md` (70 pts, 75 min, case-based)
- **Format:** 10× T/F with one-sentence justification (2 pts) + 3× case analyses (10 pts) + compare-contrast essay (20 pts) + 5-pt bonus definitions. Excellent applied-ethics format. ✅
- **Coverage:** Cosmetics/Draize case exercises 3R + utilitarian + Kantian + virtue analysis; Indigenous genomics case exercises FAIR vs. CARE + cumulative advantage; Pandemic case exercises utilitarian/Kantian + Helsinki + descriptive ethics. All tested topics are present and *integrated*, which is what a strong exam should do. ✅
- **One small caveat:** Case 3(b) refers to the *Declaration of Helsinki* "as mentioned in the Soucitně article." The Soucitně article does mention Helsinki briefly in the welfare-of-the-subject context; students should not rely solely on that article for the full Declaration text. The answer key wisely paraphrases the principle rather than quoting article numbers verbatim.
- **Verdict: hits the mark; arguably better than Test 1 because it forces theory-to-case transfer.**

### `ANSWER_KEYS.md`
- Model answers are accurate, appropriately concise, and contain the **rubric language** examiners actually use ("states a clear thesis," "anticipates one objection"). No factual errors found. ✅

## Are the guides "concise and perfect"?

- **Concise:** Yes for the audience; the guide is ~340 lines covering 4 large topics — that is the lower bound of acceptable depth, not bloat.
- **Perfect:** Now that the four factual fixes above are in, **yes — at the level expected of an undergraduate revision document.** A graduate-level guide would add: Scanlon's contractualism, Beauchamp & Childress' four principles, the Belmont Report, and a deeper treatment of the is–ought problem. These are **out of scope** for PBIO 2026 as announced and have intentionally not been added.

## Materials that are "out of topic"

Only one: **Friedman & Hendry, *Value Sensitive Design*** in `04_Normative_Theories/`. It is a valuable book but does not map to any of the announced exam topics (utilitarianism, deontology, virtue/telos, descriptive ethics, animal ethics + 3Rs, Open Science). It has been **explicitly flagged as off-syllabus** in the materials map. Suggested action: **do not delete** (useful for thesis or future ethics-of-tech courses), but **do not study for the test**.

## One-page student takeaway

If a student has only one hour left:

1. Memorize the **Comparison Table** in Part I of the study guide.
2. Memorize the **3Rs definitions** with one example each, plus the EU Directive number.
3. Memorize **FAIR**, that **FAIR ≠ Open**, and that **CARE** is the Indigenous-data complement.
4. Memorize Singer (sentience/speciesism), Regan (subject-of-a-life/abolition), Cohen (moral community).
5. Be able to *apply* utilitarian / Kantian / Aristotelian reasoning to one fresh case in 5 sentences.

That covers ~80% of the points on either practice test.
